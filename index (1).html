Does sex change what love means?

PLS 210 · Research Design Part 1 · Fall 2026 · Nazarbayev University · Pseudonym: X

The puzzle

Research question

  • 2024 pilot: 20 male NU students. Sex makes love stronger, but more experience means less “amorousness.”
  • I only asked men.

How do male and female NU undergraduates describe the link between sexual experience and love in the 2026–2027 academic year?

EmpiricalHow they describe it, not how they should live
ScopedNU undergraduates, by gender, 2026–2027
AnswerableThrough interviews
Where I stand

Positioning: interpretivist

  • Meaning, not measurement (Schwartz-Shea & Yanow, 2012).
  • “Love” and “amorousness” have no fixed definition to count.
  • I compare men’s and women’s stories, not causes.
  • Shame shapes answers, so method matters (Crawford & Popp, 2003).
What this adds

Contribution

  • Kazakhstan research: sex education, parents, surveys.
  • Double-standard research: mostly American.
  • Gap: no one has asked NU men and women how they link sex and love.
  • NU is where uyat meets global scripts.

NU sits between the two.

My lens

Analytical framework

Gendered sexual scripts under uyat. These are sensitising concepts, not fixed codes.

CulturalWhat “everyone knows”
InterpersonalWhat they do in relationships
IntrapsychicHow they explain their own feelings
Uyat (filter)What can be said, and by whom

Expectations

  • Men: experience = maturity, or less amorousness.
  • Women: trust, reputation, marriage. They’ll speak more carefully.
  • Both: a gap between their own beliefs and what their family expects.
  • Jokes, silences and language switching show where uyat works.

Positionality

Insider with men

Rapport, but they may perform masculinity.

Outsider with women

It’s uyat to talk about sex with a man.

Response: a female co-interviewer option, written answers, cameras off, and I’ll report the effect.

What working with AI helped me understand, and what I still don’t understand about my research design

Understood

  • “Does X affect Y?” and “How do people describe X and Y?” are different kinds of question.
  • My 2024 study mixed a positivist design with interpretive data.
  • My position is part of the findings.

Still unclear

  • How to compare genders without it becoming a causal comparison.
  • How many interviews are enough.
  • How to treat language switching as evidence.
